# Commissioning illustrators — practical playbook

**Purpose:** engaging a human illustrator/designer for bespoke visual work when AI-generated illustration doesn't hit the quality bar. Written when Concert Crawl pivoted #1114 (per-venue imagery) from AI-assisted to human-commissioned, but structured to be reusable for any future bespoke visual commission (event posters, marketing art, illustrated features, etc.).

**When to use this doc:**
- You're commissioning original illustration work and Fiverr/generic freelance marketplaces feel wrong for the visual quality you need
- The project is Concert Crawl-sized (~small-to-medium spend, ~20–50 pieces)
- You want editorial-grade or gig-poster-grade output, not template/vector work

**When NOT to use this doc:**
- You need one-off vector work (logos, icons) — Fiverr/99designs are actually fine for that
- You need a full-time in-house designer — different playbook (job posting, portfolio review, contracts, salary)

---

## 1. Why Fiverr / Upwork usually aren't the right venue

Fiverr and Upwork optimize for high-volume, low-price, template-shaped creative work. Signal-to-noise is terrible for editorial or illustration work at a real quality bar. The platforms select for illustrators whose business model is *volume at low margin* — which is misaligned with commissioning ~20–50 unique pieces at a quality bar that would land on a considered product page.

Skip both unless you're commissioning something genuinely templatable (product mockups, spot vector illustrations, standard iconography).

## 2. Where to look, ranked by fit for Concert Crawl-style work

### 🏆 Curated marketplaces (someone else has filtered)

- **[Working Not Working](https://workingnotworking.com)** — invite-only for illustrators/designers, so the roster is already vetted. Browse by discipline (Illustration), filter by location. Editorial and gig-poster folks well-represented. Free to browse; direct-message for commission. **Best single starting point** for editorial-grade illustration work.
- **[Contra](https://contra.com)** — freelancer-first, designer-heavy tilt. More curated than Fiverr/Upwork. You post a scoped brief; interested designers respond. The "hire" flow is less overwhelming than marketplace-browse.
- **[Cargo](https://cargo.site)** — hand-coded portfolio sites; skews artsy/editorial. Not a marketplace per se — more a directory of designers with real portfolios. Google `"Cargo illustrator [style you like]"` to shortlist.

### 🎯 Gig-poster-specific (closest fit to Concert Crawl's existing riso/poster DNA)

- **[Flatstock](https://americanposterinstitute.com/flatstock)** — the American Poster Institute's touring show of poster artists. Their roster is *the* concentrated pool of gig-poster illustrators. Any recent show's exhibitor list is a curated shortlist of ~100 poster artists who take commissions.
- **[r/gigposters](https://reddit.com/r/gigposters)** — active poster-artist community; many post work and openly take commissions. Lower-friction to reach out via Reddit DM.
- **Instagram tags:** `#gigposter`, `#screenprint`, `#risoprint` — visual-first browsing. Shortlist by "I like this specific style" then check bio for commission availability.

### 📚 Editorial-illustrator discovery (for architectural/urban/series work)

- **[AIGA Eye on Design](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org)** — feature-based; showcases individual illustrators with linked portfolios.
- **[It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com)** — UK-based but roster is international; excellent for shortlisting illustrators with a coherent style.
- **[Communication Arts](https://www.commarts.com)** illustration annual — past winners are a vetted candidate pool.
- **[Behance](https://www.behance.net)** — filter by location + discipline; browse "Illustration" category. More curated than Dribbble for illustration work (Dribbble skews UI/product).

### 📍 Denver-local starting points

- **[RiNo Art District artist directory](https://rinoartdistrict.org/artists)** — River North's artist roster; includes illustrators, muralists, printmakers.
- **[DiNK — Denver Independent Comic and Art Expo](https://www.denverindependentcomicandartexpo.com)** — annual event; artist alley roster (browsable online for recent years) is essentially a curated shortlist of local illustrators who sell/commission.
- **[Diesel Fuel Prints](https://dieselfuelprints.com)** — Denver-based screenprint shop; has collaborated with local illustrators. Could take a commission directly or point you at illustrators in their network. Existing work is riso-adjacent and venue-friendly.
- **RMCAD (Rocky Mountain College of Art and Design)** alumni pages — younger illustrators tend to be more affordable + hungrier.
- **Instagram:** `#denverillustration`, `#denverartist`, `#denvergigposter` — good for finding the current active local scene.

## 3. How to shortlist

Two portfolio filters that matter more than any others:

1. **Can they draw the subject well?** For Concert Crawl-style venue imagery: can they draw buildings? Many illustrators are character-forward and mediocre at architecture. For a different subject (concert crowds, portraits, etc.) the muscle you're checking for changes but the principle holds — the illustrator's *existing* portfolio should already demonstrate the specific competence you need.
2. **Have they done a coherent series?** N pieces that feel like siblings requires demonstrated series-work muscle. City map books, hotel/restaurant chain art, sports team collections, album series — these are the portfolio pieces that prove the illustrator can hold a style across many outputs.

**One-shot tip:** find 3–4 people whose *existing work* looks like what you'd want the finished pieces to look like. Don't brief style abstractly; commission from illustrators whose native style already fits. Trying to bend an illustrator away from their default style is expensive and often unsuccessful.

---

## 4. How to brief

Give the illustrator:

1. **The design brief** (`docs/superpowers/specs/YYYY-MM-DD-...-design-brief.md` for this project) — carries the strategic frame (what's locked, what's the aspect ratio spec, what's the accessibility bar).
2. **A mood board** — 5–8 reference images of the style you're chasing, curated from THEIR portfolio + adjacent artists you like.
3. **The subject list** — for venue imagery, the venue list + reference photos (Google Street View + any you have). For other commissions, the analogous input list.
4. **What's OUT of scope** — explicit exclusions save iteration cost later.

Skip any AI-generated reference material unless you're explicitly saying "here's what we DON'T want." Human illustrators generally react poorly to being asked to imitate AI outputs, and the reference undersells the quality bar.

## 5. Commission structure (3-phase, for medium-batch work)

De-risks the money spend and gives clean cut-points.

| Phase | What | Deliverable | Ballpark cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Style exploration** | 2–3 style directions × 1–2 sample pieces each | Style guide + samples | $500–1,500 total |
| **2. Locked style** | 1 refined sample in the chosen direction + iteration | Final template + 1 piece at production quality | $200–500 |
| **3. Batch execution** | Remaining pieces at a per-piece rate | Final art + source files | $100–300 per piece (varies by illustrator tier + complexity) |
| **4. Retainer (optional)** | Same per-piece rate for new work as it's needed | Ongoing | Per-piece |

Phase 1 lets you cut cleanly if the first illustrator doesn't hit — you're out ~$1K, not ~$10K. If Phase 1 lands well, Phase 2 formalizes; if Phase 2 lands well, Phase 3 executes the full batch.

For Concert Crawl venue imagery specifically (~20–50 venues at Phase 3 rates): **rough total spend $4K–$15K** depending on illustrator tier and per-venue rate. Retainer adds ongoing $100–300 per new venue.

## 6. Rights / legal (matters more than most product folks realize)

- **Work-for-hire (buyout)** — cleaner for a product. You own everything: web, print, social, share cards, marketing, derivative use. Higher upfront cost. Contract should specify **including layered source files** (Ai/Psd) so you can render at any resolution/composition later.
- **Licensed use** (illustrator retains ownership, you license) — cheaper upfront but restricts where/how you can use. Bad fit if illustrations will hit share cards that go to Twitter/IG — that's a common exclusion in default license terms.
- **Attribution:** even under work-for-hire, giving the illustrator a credit line on the piece's page (or a `/credits` page) is a nice touch, often stipulated by illustrators, and buys goodwill in the illustration community. Costs you nothing.
- **Term:** work-for-hire is perpetual. Some licenses have geographic (e.g., North America only) or duration limits (e.g., 3 years) — check the fine print.

For Concert Crawl scale: **work-for-hire is the recommended posture** unless the illustrator strongly prefers licensing and their rate reflects it.

## 7. Practical first move — the cold-outreach outline

Draft a **1-page project outline** you can DM to 5–10 illustrators for a *"would you be interested + rough per-piece rate?"* touch. Response data is cheap; commitment isn't required from either side.

The outline needs:

- 2-sentence what-it-is
- Subject count (e.g., ~20–50 pieces)
- Style direction (be honest: *"we're figuring it out"* is fine; naming reference styles helps)
- Commission structure you're proposing (3-phase above)
- Rights posture (work-for-hire)
- Reference to existing brand context (for Concert Crawl: link the gig-poster / shareable-card work as visual DNA)
- Rough timeline
- *"reply with rate + a couple portfolio examples of relevant series work"*

Send to 5–10 illustrators. Expect ~30–50% response rate. Shortlist to 2–3 real conversations. Commit Phase 1 spend to one.

---

## 8. Anti-patterns to avoid

- **Don't commission from a single illustrator without Phase 1** — even great illustrators can be a bad style fit for a specific project. Phase 1 is your calibration step.
- **Don't skip the style-guide phase** — jumping straight to batch execution means every piece needs individual creative direction. Style guide upfront = batch execution goes fast and cheap.
- **Don't provide AI-generated "here's the style" references** — illustrators react poorly and it undersells the quality bar you actually want.
- **Don't rush contract negotiation** — work-for-hire vs licensed use is a real difference. If the illustrator's contract skews restrictive, negotiate before Phase 1 starts, not after Phase 3.
- **Don't forget source files** — a PNG at fixed resolution can bite you when a new surface (say, share cards at a new size) needs the art re-rendered. Source files (Ai/Psd/Sketch) are worth requesting explicitly in the contract.
